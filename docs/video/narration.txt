GovFlow demo: narration, one line per beat (about 140 words a minute).
Timestamps are the beats of docs/video/govflow_demo_captioned.mp4, written by scripts/make_demo_video.mjs.

[0:00.0 - 0:10.4] Beat 1 (24 words, 138.5 words a minute)
GovFlow is one AI agent for government case processing. Today, files are retyped by hand, checked from memory, and citizens cannot track their application.

[0:10.4 - 0:37.5] Beat 2 (65 words, 143.6 words a minute)
A citizen can check a document before applying. Here is a synthetic ownership record, a clear scan. Each required field is either found or marked as unreadable, and nothing is saved: the file is checked in memory and thrown away. Now a blurred phone photo of the same record. It is only partly readable, and the page says in plain words what to do next.

[0:37.5 - 0:50.5] Beat 3 (32 words, 147.7 words a minute)
A citizen tracks an application with a reference and code. This approved case shows the amount and its basis. The page reads in Hindi too. A waiting case lists what is missing.

[0:50.5 - 1:26.7] Beat 4 (65 words, 107.9 words a minute)
A clerk opens a new land case and uploads its documents. They are synthetic: one ownership record image and four short printed records. The language model reads each document and returns every field with a confidence score. Waiting time is sped up on screen, and marked. The model only reads; it decides nothing. Then the agent runs the checks, the eligibility rules and the amount.

[1:26.7 - 1:52.4] Beat 5 (64 words, 149.4 words a minute)
The officer opens the new case. One plain sentence says where it stands, then the total and what needs attention. The checks are fixed rules in the scheme file, not the model, and each shows its result and version. Each field shows how sure the reading was. The amount is worked out in steps: area, value and multiplier, then solatium and the additional amount.

[1:52.4 - 2:28.1] Beat 6 (76 words, 127.6 words a minute)
On LA-18, a tenant entry on the record of rights is flagged, citing the Goa tenancy and mundkar Acts. The model writes the explanation, but every figure in it comes from the tools. On LA-06, where a document has expired, the officer previews a notice to the citizen in English and Hindi. Back on LA-18, the officer confirms each money field, records the resolution, and approves. A clerk sees no decision controls. Only an officer decides.

[2:28.1 - 2:47.6] Beat 7 (47 words, 145.0 words a minute)
An administrator sees four headline numbers, read from the system, then one chart, and more detail only when asked. The case list is for officers and clerks. The rule simulator changes one parameter on the synthetic cases and shows which would change. It never edits the rules.

[2:47.6 - 3:01.4] Beat 8 (33 words, 143.4 words a minute)
Every action goes into a hash-chained audit log, and it checks out intact. Duplicate claims and open mutations go to review, never rejection. Four schemes, one engine, each defined in a rules file.

[3:01.4 - 3:11.4] Beat 9 (25 words, 150.0 words a minute)
These results come from our own tests on synthetic data. Legal values stay placeholders until the offices confirm. A scripted walkthrough of the live prototype.
